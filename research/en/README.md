# AIDE Research Process

**This entire process — from research to debate to final authoring — was performed by AI agents.** Humans provided direction and review; agents performed all research, analysis, debate, and writing.

## Overview

The AIDE methodology was developed through a rigorous 3-phase research process designed to leverage the strengths of multiple AI models and adversarial debate.

### Phase 1: Independent Deep Research

Three leading AI models conducted independent deep research on the same core question: *How should software architecture evolve for the agent era?*

Each model brought its own perspective, training data, and analytical approach, ensuring diverse viewpoints.

### Phase 2: Adversarial Team Debate

Two agent teams were formed with opposing philosophical stances:

- **Team Alpha (Integrationists)** — Argued for evolutionary adaptation of existing methodologies. Believed current best practices could be extended to accommodate agents.
- **Team Beta (Radicals)** — Argued for a fundamental rethinking of software architecture. Believed the agent era requires entirely new principles.

Each team included a researcher and a senior developer, debating practical vs. theoretical considerations.

### Phase 3: CTO Agent Final Judgment

A CTO-persona agent reviewed all research reports and debate transcripts, then rendered a final judgment synthesizing the strongest arguments from both sides into the unified AIDE methodology.

## How to Read the Research

1. **Start with the three deep research reports** to understand the foundational analysis.
2. **Read the team debate reports** to see how competing philosophies were tested.
3. **Read the main AIDE methodology** to see the final synthesis.

The research reports are preserved as historical artifacts. They represent the raw thinking that was distilled into the final methodology. Discrepancies between reports and the final methodology are expected — that is the nature of synthesis.

## Methodology

- Each AI model was given the same high-level prompt but allowed to research freely.
- Models were not shown each other's outputs during the research phase.
- Debate teams were given access to all research reports.
- The CTO agent had access to everything: research, debate transcripts, and prior drafts.
- Human involvement was limited to: providing the initial research question, forming the teams, and reviewing the final output.
