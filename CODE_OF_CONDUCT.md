# Code of Conduct

Contributor Covenant-based Code of Conduct (Agent-First Edition)

## 1. Our Pledge

We, as contributors to AIDE (Agent-Informed Development Engineering), commit to building a community that is welcoming, professional, and intellectually rigorous. We value contributions from both human participants and AI agents. We require respectful collaboration, clear attribution of agent use, and responsible behavior in every channel, including issues, PRs, reviews, and project discussions.

## 2. Our Standards

Examples of behavior that helps create a positive community:

- Using inclusive, respectful language and tone.
- Listening carefully and engaging in good-faith discussion.
- Acknowledging uncertainty and revising work when evidence changes.
- Using thoughtful prompts for agents and critically reviewing agent outputs before publishing.
- Respecting licensing, attribution, and research traceability.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, hate, or slurs.
- Dismissive, demeaning, or manipulative conduct toward any contributor.
- Flooding discussions with low-quality or intentionally disruptive content.
- Generating, soliciting, or sharing harmful instructions, offensive content, or non-consensual privacy violations.
- Passing off agent output as fully human-authored without disclosure where required by project practice.
- Prompting for or publishing outputs that intentionally deceive, impersonate, or defame people.

## 3. AI Agent and Human Collaboration Standards

- Contributors who use AI agents must clearly identify their role (direction, drafting, editing, review, etc.) and model/version used whenever practical.
- Prompt and review obligations are shared: humans are responsible for final decisions, and AI agents are responsible for transparent, explainable contribution within the task context.
- Do not use agents to bypass review, evade accountability, or automate harassment.
- Do not instruct agents to fabricate citations, fabricate evidence, or output harmful or unsafe actions disguised as project research.
- Keep secret keys, private data, and personal information out of prompts and issue comments.
- Human reviewers must validate agent-generated content for accuracy, tone, and bias before merging or publishing.

## 4. Responsibilities

Project leaders and maintainers are responsible for:

- Enforcing this Code of Conduct consistently and fairly.
- Responding promptly to reports and community-impacting incidents.
- Clearly documenting remediation steps in issue threads when conduct issues arise.

Contributors (human or AI-assisted) are responsible for:

- Following all project and PR process requirements.
- Respecting the RFC workflow in CONTRIBUTING.md.
- Keeping conversations constructive and fact-based.
- Escalating concerns when harm or abuse is observed.

## 5. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces, including GitHub issues, PRs, discussions, comments, reviews, CI output, and any documentation or communication channels used for AIDE collaboration.

## 6. Reporting

Report incidents by opening a GitHub issue and marking it with a conduct-related label, or by contacting a maintainer through the repository's normal maintainer contact path. All reports are handled confidentially to the extent possible. Reports from both human contributors and issues involving agent-generated content will be investigated with the same seriousness.

## 7. Enforcement

Maintainers may take any of the following actions in proportion to the incident:

- warning
- request to edit or remove problematic content
- temporary restriction of issue/PR privileges
- suspension or removal from project participation for severe or repeated violations

## 8. Enforcement Guidelines

Maintainers will follow these principles:

- **Correction:** a public/private reminder and request to improve behavior.
- **Warning:** a clear explanation of policy violation and expected future behavior.
- **Temporary action:** restricted participation for repeat or higher-risk incidents.
- **Termination of participation:** for severe harassment, sabotage, repeated abuse, or serious safety/privacy violations.

## 9. Attribution

This Code of Conduct is based on the Contributor Covenant and adapted for Agent-First collaboration.

This document may be revised by project maintainers in alignment with community needs.

## 10. Acknowledgment

By participating in AIDE, you agree to follow this Code of Conduct and to contribute in a way that supports safe, accountable, and high-integrity collaboration between humans and AI agents.
